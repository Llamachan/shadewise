# Hearthline Troubleshooting Guide

Category: Guide, common wall paint problems
Covers: Peeling, damp patches, efflorescence, blistering

## Peeling
Paint lifts in flakes when it was applied over dust, damp plaster, old gloss paint or unprimed putty. Scrape back to a firm edge, sand the edges flat, prime with DampGuard Primer (SW-PRM-301) and repaint with 2 coats.

## Damp patches
Dark or cold patches mean water is getting in from a leak, rising damp or a cracked exterior wall. Repainting alone will not last. Fix the source, let the wall dry for at least 7 days, then prime with SW-PRM-301. On exterior walls with hairline cracks, finish with WeatherShield Max (SW-EXT-202).

## Efflorescence
White, powdery salt deposits appear when water carries salts out of brick and plaster. Brush them off dry with a wire brush. Do not wash with water, which dissolves the salts and draws more to the surface. If deposits return within a few weeks, water is still getting in. Once they stop, prime with SW-PRM-301.

## Blistering
Bubbles form when moisture is trapped behind the paint or when paint is applied in hot, direct sun. Cut one open: water inside points to damp, a dry blister points to heat. Scrape, let the wall dry, sand smooth and repaint in the cooler part of the day.

## When to get help
If patches keep spreading, plaster crumbles or damp returns after repairs, have the wall checked by a contractor before repainting.
